# Startup Thesis and Buyer

## Product

The proposed product is an **Agent Purchase Assurance Layer**.

It sits between an agent's business request and the payment rail. It converts the request into a testable mandate, evaluates the proposed purchase, invokes the payment provider, verifies delivery, and produces an audit record.

The simplest positioning is:

> Rain lets agents pay. We prove the agent was authorized, selected an acceptable purchase, and received what it paid for.

## Buyer map

| Role | Typical person |
|---|---|
| Delegates the task | Sales, marketing, procurement, finance, or operations employee |
| Performs the purchase | AI agent |
| Daily operational user | Finance operations, procurement, or GTM operations |
| Technical champion | Head of AI, automation lead, platform engineer, or fintech product lead |
| Economic buyer | CFO, Controller, Head of Procurement, or COO |
| Risk approver/blocker | CISO, fraud/risk lead, compliance, privacy, or legal |
| Distribution partner | Rain, card platform, agent wallet, procurement platform, or agent builder |

## Initial ideal customer

The best initial customer is an AI-native mid-market company or technology-enabled service business that:

- is already allowing agents to procure software, data, APIs, cloud resources, or vendor services;
- has repeated delegated transactions rather than one experimental purchase;
- uses corporate cards or stablecoin infrastructure;
- still relies on manual approvals, receipt collection, and reconciliation;
- cares about duplicate subscriptions, vendor quality, privacy, or audit evidence.

Individual consumers are not the recommended first market. Their urgency, transaction volume, and willingness to pay are lower.

## Best first wedge

Start with software, API, data, and digital-service purchases.

These are attractive because:

- transactions are frequent and measurable;
- budgets and approved-vendor policies already exist;
- digital delivery can be verified automatically;
- duplicate subscriptions and unused credits are common;
- the buyer already has procurement, finance, fraud, or accounting budget;
- they avoid the physical-delivery complexity of general shopping.

## Commercial model

Possible pricing units include:

- enterprise platform fee;
- active purchasing agent;
- assured transaction;
- spend under management;
- verified savings or prevented loss.

No willingness-to-pay interviews or validated pricing data were completed. These are hypotheses, not established market prices.

## First-customer motion

1. Recruit AI-native GTM agencies, coding-agent companies, or operations teams already running automated purchasing experiments.
2. Offer a narrow pilot around one repeated digital purchase.
3. Measure approval time, duplicate spend prevented, failed delivery detected, and reconciliation effort.
4. Use Rain ecosystem companies and the hackathon network as design partners.
5. Later sell the API through agent platforms, card providers, and procurement systems.

## Main strategic risk

Rain, Stripe, Sponge, or another infrastructure provider could add deeper policy features. The defensible product therefore cannot be only a dashboard over Rain controls.

The moat would need to become:

- vendor and product intelligence;
- historical price and quality evidence;
- mandate and policy evaluation data;
- delivery-verification integrations;
- cross-rail reconciliation;
- a trusted audit and liability standard.
